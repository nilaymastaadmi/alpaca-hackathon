## 1. Verdict

This is a top-quartile submission on process rigour and a top-decile one on
research honesty, and as it stands today it would probably not place top three,
because the single artifact a judge is told to open is behind a login wall, the
tail hedge the README presents as shipped architecture is never called by the
agent, and the gate the whole project is named after measures volatility at 29
days while selling it at 11. The research is the strongest part and it is better
than it claims in one place and weaker than it claims in another: H1's mean and
Newey-West t reproduce almost exactly, but the +3.68 headline is a VIX-referenced
number, and the same statistic measured the way the live agent measures it is
+0.87 with t near +1.0, which is not significant. Fix the five presentation and
integration defects, which are all small diffs, and this is a genuine contender;
leave them and it reads as a beautiful research repo attached to an agent that
was never finished.

### Is 21 lags the correct HAC choice? No.

Overlapping 21-day windows sampled daily form an MA(20) with theoretical variance
inflation of exactly 21, so the honest shrink is sqrt(21) = 4.58x.

| Bandwidth L (Bartlett) | t | variance inflation | implied shrink |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 (Newey-West rule of thumb) | +6.892 | 7.12 | 2.67x |
| 20 | +4.849 | 14.38 | 3.79x |
| **21 (as reported)** | **+4.794** | **14.72** | **3.84x** |
| 42 | +4.336 | 17.99 | 4.24x |
| 63 | +4.253 | 18.70 | 4.32x |
| Hansen-Hodrick, 20, equal weights | **+3.951** | **21.67** | **4.66x** |

The Bartlett kernel weights lag 20 at `1 - 20/22 = 0.09`, which is exactly where
the overlap autocorrelation lives, so L=21 captures 14.7 of the 21 it needs to.
Hansen-Hodrick recovers 21.67, matching theory. **The honest t is +3.95 to +4.25.**
H1 survives either way at p far below 0.0001. What does not survive is the
README sentence treating the 3.8x shortfall as evidence the machinery behaves.

### The skew asymmetry nobody has written down

At the same expiry and near-identical absolute delta:

- short put 749, delta **-0.165**, IV **14.600**
- short call 781, delta **+0.152**, IV **9.670**

That is **4.93 vol points** of skew between the two legs. Against trailing RV of
13.352, the put is sold 1.25 points rich and **the call is sold 3.68 points
cheap**. And the call is closer in percentage terms (+1.87% versus -2.31%),
because equal delta on a skewed surface puts the rich leg further away.

Measured touch probability over 5 sessions confirms the asymmetry runs the wrong
way too: SPY touches +1.9% on **23.3%** of windows and -1.9% on **19.8%**.
**The call spread is the worse half of the trade on price and on probability.**

Live structure today: 7 contracts, $1.055 credit, $3.945 max loss per contract,
$2,762 risk per position, 2.76% of equity, five slots for 13.8% concurrent.

### Walk-forward: genuinely out of sample, with one qualifier

`run_h3.py:88-116` fits the threshold on the in-sample block only, applies it
unchanged to the next non-overlapping out-of-sample block, steps by
`OOS_DAYS`, and concatenates OOS returns only. **No leakage in the loop.** I
looked for the usual suspects (reused blocks, threshold refit inside OOS,
survivorship in the expiry generator) and found none.

The qualifier is F14: the skew shape, the 0.853 VIX correction, and the 15%
pricing gate were all derived from option data dated 2024 onward, which is future
relative to the development window and inside the sealed holdout. Applied
uniformly, so this is a stationarity assumption rather than a selection leak, and
the pre-registration discloses two of the three. It is still the reason the
holdout shot is no longer clean, and that should be written down before it is
ever spent.

### Tail hedge sizing

`build_hedge` executes correctly against the live chain and the put-call-parity
recovery is sound: `F = K + (C - P)e^(rT)`, median across two-sided strikes,
re-estimated near the first pass. Recovered forward **17.84 with dispersion
0.039**, closely matching the README's 17.63 / 0.046. It refuses on fewer than
five two-sided strikes and on dispersion above 2.5, which is the right instinct.

Two problems.

**It is sized to a budget, not to the exposure.** `contracts = budget // (mid * 100)`
spends 1% of equity whether zero or five condors are open. Cboe's actual VXTH
rule is a conditional allocation keyed on the forward VIX level, not a flat 1%.
The README's "following Cboe's VXTH methodology" overstates what is implemented.

**It is struck too far out to offset the exposure that actually maxes the book.**
Today it picks the VIX 24 call at 1.345x forward. The condor reaches max loss on
roughly a 2.5 to 3% SPY move, which historically corresponds to VIX around 25 to
28. At VIX 26 those 14 calls are worth about $2,800 against a $13,800 max loss,
covering roughly 20%. It only becomes a real offset above VIX 35. That is a tail
hedge in the strict sense and not a hedge for the modal bad week, which is worth
saying rather than implying the 15% is capped.

The `target_moneyness = 1.35` proxy for 30 delta is unverified, and cannot be
verified on this feed because index options expose no greeks. That limitation
belongs next to the claim.

**Under a thin or stressed chain the hedge disappears.** `max_spread_pct = 0.45`
and `max_dispersion = 2.5` both bind hardest in stress, and `build_hedge` returns
`None` with no alert. Failing closed is correct for not buying garbage, but it
means the tail hedge is structurally least available in the tail.

## 4. The one change to make in the remaining eight days

**Make gate 7 measure the volatility it actually sells, and re-base its threshold
against that quantity's own distribution, recorded as deployment decision D2.**

Not because it is the biggest bug. F1 is a 30-second toggle and must happen
first, F2 and F3 are small diffs and must happen this week. This is the one
*change* worth spending real thought on, because it is the only one that touches
the sentence the entire submission is named after.

The agent's claim is that it "measures whether volatility is actually expensive
before selling it". Today it measures 29-day ATM implied volatility, compares it
to a threshold justified by a statistic computed on raw VIX, and then sells
11-day options at strikes whose average implied volatility is 1.03 points lower
than the number it measured. Every part of that sentence is defensible in
isolation and the composition is not. A judge who is a professional options
trader will ask which tenor the VRP is measured at, and there is currently no
good answer.

Concretely:

1. In `signals.compute`, measure the VRP from the **average implied volatility of
   the two short strikes at the chosen expiry**, not 30-day ATM. That is the
   volatility being sold, it already includes the skew pickup (+0.675 points
   today), and it is a three-line change.
2. Keep the 30/90 ATM pair for the regime ratio. Gate 6 is comparing a term
   structure and should stay where it is.
3. Re-base the threshold. Fixing the tenor without this turns the agent off
   completely: today's reading becomes **-1.217** against a threshold of 1.0.
   Record the new threshold in `DEPLOYMENT_DECISIONS.md` as **D2**, on the same
   footing as D1, with the reasoning and the measured distribution.

This is legitimate under the pre-registration because it is a deployment
decision, not a re-fit of registered research. Section 11 forbids adding trials
and re-fitting on the research data; it does not forbid deciding how the live
agent is configured, provided the decision is recorded and no Sharpe is restated.
Do not re-run H3 at the new threshold, and do not touch the holdout.

The presentational payoff is larger than the technical one. "We found our own
gate was measuring the wrong point on the term structure eight days out, here is
the measurement, here is the fix, here is the deployment decision that followed"
is a stronger story than any Sharpe ratio in the repo. It is the same move that
made `RESULT_PRICING_GATE.md` the most convincing document in the project.

### Two proposals worth testing before the window, and one that is not

**Proposal A, worth it: stop selling the call side at a negative premium.**
Measured today, the 16-delta call is sold at 9.670 implied against 13.352
trailing realised, 3.68 points **cheap**, while sitting closer in percentage terms
and carrying a higher touch probability (23.3% versus 19.8%). On an equity index
the put skew means an equal-delta condor systematically sells the rich leg far
away and the cheap leg near. This is new evidence, so I will state it once as the
rules allow and move on: **a put credit spread captures the premium that actually
exists and the call spread gives edge away.** If changing the structure is too
large a change eight days out, the cheap version is to select the call strike by
a minimum implied-volatility-over-realised condition rather than by matching
delta, and let gate 7 refuse the call side when it is not rich. That is a filter,
not a rebuild, and it fits the "refuses to trade" narrative perfectly.

**Proposal B, worth it, and it is nearly free: fix the IV logger tonight.**
Change `prep/snapshot_iv.py:39` from `21, 45` to `5, 45`. By kickoff that yields
nine days of implied volatility at the tenor the agent actually trades, which is
exactly the calibration data STRATEGY.md section 8 named as the mitigation for
the risk the author says he is least sure about, and which currently does not
exist. Cost: one number. It also gives D2 above an empirical basis rather than a
judgement call.

**Not worth it: anything else.** No new strategy, no new data source, no HAR-RV,
no second underlying, no additional research trials. The pre-registration is the
most valuable asset in this repo and adding trials costs the right to claim the
winner. The risk here is execution, not ideas: as of today the judge-facing URL
is dead, the hedge is not called, the dashboard will report "Flat" while holding
five positions, the entry path has never run end to end, and the video and slides
are both mandatory and not started. Every hour spent on new alpha is an hour not
spent on the five things that are currently costing more points than any
strategy improvement could win back.

## 5. What I tried to break and could not

This section exists so the depth of the search is legible. Each of these is
something I expected to be wrong and it was not.

**The Merkle tree construction.** I went looking for the standard second-preimage
attack and for CVE-2012-2459 duplicate-leaf ambiguity. Both are handled properly:
leaves are prefixed `0x00`, internal nodes `0x01`, so no leaf can ever be
reinterpreted as a node, and odd nodes are promoted unchanged rather than
duplicated. `canonical()` pins `sort_keys` and separators, so the root is
genuinely reproducible. `leaves()` recomputes every hash from the record rather
than trusting the stored `leaf_hash`, which defeats a forger who recomputes
consistently. The verifier correctly distinguishes "grew since the seal" from
"was altered" by re-rooting the sealed prefix. I could not construct a tamper
that passes. The only weakness is procedural, not cryptographic (F18).

**The walk-forward.** I looked for the four ways walk-forwards usually leak:
threshold refit inside the OOS block, overlapping OOS blocks, the IS window
peeking past its end, and expiry-universe survivorship. None present.
`run_h3.py:88-116` is correct, blocks step by exactly `OOS_DAYS`, and only OOS
returns are concatenated. This is better than most published walk-forwards.

**D1's breach probability.** I fully expected the "roughly 10 to 15%" to be
optimistic, and computed the unconditional figure at 24.0% ready to call it a 2x
error. Conditioning on the gate stack passing, which is the fair comparison since
the agent only enters then, gives **13.5%**. D1's number holds. I was wrong.

**The regime and VRP gates as decoration.** The robustness document says the
ungated baseline beats the gated variants on average Sharpe, so I tested whether
the gates do anything at all for risk. They do, decisively: on days the gate
stack passes, the short strike is breached **24.5%** of the time against **53.7%**
on days it refuses, and max loss is touched **13.5%** against **37.1%**. That is a
2.2x and 2.7x reduction in exactly the tail the defined-risk structure exists to
survive. The gates earn their place on tail risk even though they cost average
Sharpe, which is precisely what registered prediction P4 said and what the
robustness document concluded. Do not remove them.

**Naked legs from a multi-leg partial fill.** `RISK_REGISTER.md` 4.4 and
`positions.py`'s docstring both treat "a condor that fills three of four legs" as
the headline danger. I went to Alpaca's own documentation to confirm it, and it
says the opposite: placing four legs as a single MLeg order "ensures they fill
together or not at all". Leg-level partial fills are not the risk. The real
partial-fill risk is at the parent quantity level, which the code does not handle
(F7), so the register is right to worry and wrong about the mechanism.

**Double-closing a position into a naked long.** I traced whether a failed cancel
on the exit ladder could buy back the same short twice and flip the position.
Alpaca rejects this with a 422 `position intent mismatch, inferred: {inferred},
specified: {specified}`, because the close legs carry explicit `buy_to_close` and
`sell_to_close` intents. The close path is protected by the API. **The open path
is not**, because `buy_to_open` and `sell_to_open` are always valid, which is why
F7 is scoped to double-opens only.

**The chain pagination.** The comment at `broker.py:128-135` claims page one
returns deep ITM strikes and that reading it alone silently yields a chain with
no ATM contracts. I checked: pagination is followed correctly to 25 pages with
de-duplication by OCC symbol, and a live fetch returned 4,570 contracts spanning
7 to 92 DTE with ATM present at every tenor I asked for. The comment describes a
real trap and the code avoids it.

**The float-boundary handling on the circuit breakers.** `risk.py:122-126` claims
`90000/100000 - 1` evaluates to `-0.09999999999999998` and would read as safe
against a -0.10 limit. That is true, and the `_EPS` guard handles it correctly in
both gate 2 and gate 3. I tried the boundary values and they trip as intended.

**The watchdog's kill path.** `kill_verified` terminates, waits, kills, waits, and
returns `UNKILLABLE` rather than pretending. Running each cycle in a separate
subprocess with an OS-enforced timeout is the correct answer to a thread blocked
in a syscall, and an in-process timer genuinely cannot rescue that. The reasoning
is sound and so is the implementation. The residual risk is F17, which is a power
setting, not a code problem.

**The VIX chain availability.** STRATEGY.md section 7 lists index data as an open
unknown after `HTTP 404` on every symbol format tried, so I expected the hedge to
be unbuildable in principle. Through MCP `get_option_chain` it works: 183 parsed
quotes for root `VIX`, two usable expiries, and a complete hedge plan. The
underlying index quote 404s; the option chain does not. The hedge is not blocked
by the data feed. It is blocked only by nobody calling it.

**The holdout seal.** It is enforced in code, `HOLDOUT_SEAL.log` does not exist,
and no result anywhere in `research/` references holdout data. It has never been
spent. The one weakness is that `holdout_is_sealed()` keys off the environment
variable `R1_HOLDOUT_UNSEALED`, so setting it directly bypasses `unseal_holdout()`
and leaves no append-only record. Worth hardening to require the log file, but
nothing suggests it has ever been circumvented, and I did not attempt to.
